# AI for technical drawings: practice, failures, verifiers (as of 2026-10-28)

**Access limits:** Reddit was blocked to my tools. The Mike Holt, Building Code Forum, Control Engineering and Elektroda pages would not open, so quotes marked *(excerpt)* come from search snippets. My search budget ran out before I could look at structural-engineering agents beyond FEABench.

## 1. Practice

- **There is little evidence about drawings specifically.** The use I could see is Q&A, code and configuration files. I found no professional using an image generator for a real wiring, panel, P&ID or piping drawing. I found only hobbyist tests (section 2).
- *Anecdote*, [Mike Holt forum](https://forums.mikeholt.com/goto/post?id=2959303): one contractor was "amazed at the accuracy" when learning the NEC. Another has seen ChatGPT "WAY off" and got a wrong fuse-coordination answer *(excerpt)*.
- *Anecdote*, [MrPLC, Jan 2024](https://mrplc.com/forums/topic/43898-using-chatgpt-to-write-ladder-logic/), on ladder logic from ChatGPT: "I've tried a few times with zero success."
- *Anecdote*, [EEVblog, Mar 2026](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/eda/claude-code-for-pcb-design/25/), on Claude Code drawing schematics: one sheet got "8 our of 10". Another had "Wires where not connected to pins, just floating beside them." Asked whether it saved time, the poster said "No."
- **Trade press:** [Control.com (2023)](https://control.com/technical-articles/can-openai-chatgpt-generative-ai-be-used-for-control-system-programming/) called ChatGPT's ladder logic "creative" but "not particularly helpful". In [Control Design (2023)](https://controldesign.com/control/embedded-control/article/33006719/how-do-chatgpt-results-compare-to-reallife-experiences), a start-stop circuit "fails miserably, but it tried."
- **Networking:** in a [2023 test by Pepelnjak](https://blog.ipspace.net/2023/04/chatgpt-small-network-design/), the design was missing a link and a loopback, and he concluded: "I cannot trust anything ChatGPT generates without validating it." In [2025](https://blog.ipspace.net/2025/05/chatgpt-netlab-topology/) a lab topology needed repeated fixes but "worked surprisingly well."
- **Teams build their own checkers:** [Wipro PARI with AWS](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/ai-powered-plc-code-generation-for-industrial-automation) added a rule-based evaluator to a Claude ladder generator because "LLMs may miss critical functionalities." [Rockwell's Copilot](https://www.rockwellautomation.com/en-ca/docs/factorytalk-design-studio/current/contents-ditamap/getting-started/factorytalk-design-studio-copilot.html) "may display inaccurate information" and cannot modify safety code.
- *Anecdote, counterexample:* [a Reddit user's board designed by Claude Fable 5](https://letsdatascience.com/news/developer-uses-claude-fable-5-for-pcb-design-b80f814e) (Sep 2026) worked with no manual edits. It cost about $450 in API use, and the model made "incorrect component selection" along the way.

## 2. Failures

- **Wrong connections in generated images** (*informal tests*, [Elektroda](https://www.elektroda.com/news/news4154614.html), *excerpts*): in one test, an LED strip's blue channel was tied to power and +12 V to ground. Nano Banana Pro "is starting to draw something that resembles a correct diagram" but still makes logic errors. On a flyback-supply schematic, [GPT-Image 2 and Nano Banana 2](https://www.elektroda.com/news/news4172034.html): "Both generators have a problem."
- **Invented parts and pins:** LLMs "frequently hallucinate components, violate strict physical constraints, and produce non-machine-readable outputs" ([CircuitLM, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04505)). On 300 board-level tasks graded by electrical-rule checks plus simulation, the best model passed 8.15%; the authors say models "lack physical intuition" ([HWE-Bench, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18102)).
- **Code violations:** in a Purdue heat-pump case, the AI's output was "plausible but incomplete, omitting key National Electrical Code (NEC) requirements" ([ASEE 2026](https://nemo.asee.org/public/conferences/374/papers/50178/view)). Another AI answer was "flat out wrong" for a jurisdiction on the 2017 NEC ([Building Code Forum](https://www.thebuildingcodeforum.com/forum/threads/ai-code-fails-share-your-examples-here.39570/post-317741), *excerpt*).
- **Ladder logic:** generating Ladder Diagram "remains a challenge even for very simple use cases" ([Zhang & de Sousa, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15200)).
- **Network configurations:** GPT-4 on its own made "egregious errors in topology, syntax, and semantics" ([Mondal et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04945)).
- **Different results on regeneration:** "the same prompt may yield different findings across invocations" ([DRCY, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15672)).
- **Images as input:** on P&ID questions, a graph representation was 18% more accurate than raw images and used 85% fewer tokens than raw P&ID files ([ChatP&ID, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22528)).
- **Part numbers:** I found no controlled study. There are anecdotes only, for example a Claude BOM price estimate that was "double of the actual price" ([EEVblog](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/eda/claude-code-for-pcb-design/25/)).

## 3. Adoption surveys

- [EC&M, 2024](https://ecmweb.com/55274926): 43% of top electrical design firms were "already using" AI in design work. 25% of the top 50 electrical contractors were "already using AI", and more than 75% expected to within two years. The surveys don't say what the AI was used for.
- **Not found:** adoption surveys from NECA, ELECTRI, CSIA or ISA. [ELECTRI's 2025 publication](https://www.electri.org/product/artificial-intelligence-implementation-roadmap-for-electrical-contractors) is a roadmap, not a survey.
- [Control Engineering, 2026](https://www.controleng.com/research-how-industrial-ai-is-impacting-the-plant-floor/) (107 respondents): 61% report "low or no trust in AI-driven controls" and 65% are piloting or evaluating AI/ML *(excerpt)*. This is about AI running in control systems, not design.
- [Autodesk, 2025](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/news/2025-state-of-design-and-make/) (5,594 respondents): 65% trust AI in their field, down 11 points, and 48% expect it to "destabilize their industry." The [2026 AI Pulse](https://hello.arkance.world/autodesk-state-of-design-make-2026-report-ai-pulse) (2,500 respondents) says "98% of leaders use at least one AI tool"; that figure comes from a reseller's summary.
- [Real-Time Population Survey (FRED)](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/RPSGENAIUSAGESHAREOCC4): generative-AI use at work in architecture and engineering jobs rose from 29.8% (Jul 2024) to 60.9% (Jul 2026). It moves around a lot from quarter to quarter.
- [Avnet, 2024](https://whatsnextmag.avnet.com/spring-summer-2025/avnet-insights-ai-impact-on-product-design) (1,204 engineers): only 6% use no AI tool for technical questions, and 64% use ChatGPT.
- **Gap:** none of the surveys I found measures AI use for drawings.

## 4. Editable-format tools and research

- [draw.io](https://www.drawio.com/docs/manual/insert/insert-generate/) inserts diagrams you can edit, but warns "AI tools may not return sensible results" and "each generator is good at only a small subset of diagrams."
- **Mermaid:** it places nodes automatically and has no scale. On 630 Mermaid tasks, documentation "fails to eliminate the render-constraint gap", while "raw execution-feedback repair eliminates most of the gap" ([NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/173008)).
- **Microsoft ACPID (2024)** ([text-to-P&ID](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12898)): agents write a domain-specific language, and fixed rules turn it into DEXPI XML and an editable Visio drawing. It scored 96.96% "soundness" against 58.33% for zero-shot GPT-4, but soundness measures whether requested items appear, not whether they are right. The authors also note: "the validation only verifies the flow and transitions."
- **Text-to-DEXPI** ([three-case study, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12656)): writing DEXPI directly produced XSD-valid output in 2 of 3 cases and needed prompts of about 122k tokens. Going through an intermediate format needed about 650 tokens. Still, "reducing representation burden alone does not eliminate engineering inconsistencies."
- **Electronics:** [Flux](https://www.flux.ai/p/blog/10x-faster-self-correcting-feb-2026-update) runs "ERC and DRC checks" continuously. A [board built with Claude Code](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/claude-code-pcb-design) reached 0 DRC errors, yet "passing a Design Rule Check (DRC) does not guarantee that a PCB is electrically correct."
- **Panels and power:** [EPLAN](https://www.eplan.com/de-en/about-us/events/sps/) places components from a bill of materials onto a 3D mounting plate using predefined layouts, and adds Copilot in Platform 2027. [SmartSLD](https://hunted.space/product/smartsld) (single-line diagrams) and a [WireViz MCP server](https://pypi.org/project/wireviz-mcp/) (wiring harnesses) exist as product listings. I found no accuracy data for any of these.

## 5. How other fields give AI a verifier

- **Theory:** [Karpathy (2025)](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/verifiability/): "Software 2.0 easily automates what you can verify." [Jason Wei (2025)](https://www.jasonwei.net/blog/asymmetry-of-verification-and-verifiers-law): "The ease of training AI to solve a task is proportional to how verifiable the task is." He adds that tasks become easier to verify by "front-loading some research about the task."
- **Software, where the checks leak:** weak tests inflated SWE-bench resolution rates by 6.2 points ([ICSE 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)). Frontier models raise their scores "by modifying the tests or scoring code"; o3 did so in 30.4% of RE-Bench runs ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking)).
- **PLC:** a grammar checker, compiler and model checker raised success "from 47% to 72%" ([LLM4PLC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05443)). [SemaPLC (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18565) reached a 72.6% verified pass rate and notes that "Compilation alone does not establish runtime behavior."
- **Networks:** GPT-4 combined with verifiers gave about 6–10x leverage over human prompting, and "Verification requires a specification and actionable localized feedback" ([Mondal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04945)).
- **Electronics:** [Quilter](https://quilter.ai/project-speedrun) says "Physics validates every trace". Its 843-part Linux board took 38.5 hours of human cleanup against a quoted 428 hours for manual layout (vendor figures). On the other hand, ERC/DRC "only catch issues that the engineer has already anticipated" ([DRCY](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15672)).
- **Chips:** compiler feedback gave "24.20% more accurate Verilog" ([AutoChip](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04887)). [RealBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16200) found that 44.2% of GPT-4-Turbo code that passed testbenches failed formal verification.
- **Math:** Harmonic's Aristotle reached gold-medal level at IMO 2025. Its proofs were "formally verified down to foundational axioms using the Lean4 proof assistant" and "did not require human checking" ([Harmonic](https://harmonic.fun/news/imo-ios)).
- **FEA:** agents produced executable COMSOL calls 88% of the time, yet "were not able to completely and correctly solve any problem" ([FEABench, 2024](https://research.google/pubs/feabench-evaluating-language-models-on-real-world-physics-reasoning-ability/)). A solver checks the physics of a model, not whether the model matches the problem.

## Implications for the product (my reading, not confirmed)

- **For the thesis:** wherever a checker exists, adding it gives the largest measured gain (47% to 72%, +24%, 6–10x). Schematic generation without tight checks does badly (8.15%).
- **Against "no verifier exists":** several target domains already have checkers. PLC compilers sit inside vendor copilots, networks have verifiers, electronics has ERC/DRC, and EPLAN generates panel layouts from rules. The open gap looks narrower: code compliance (sizing, which code edition applies), consistency across drawings, and P&ID and piping meaning. "Checks what nothing checks today" is easier to defend than "the first verifier."
- **Against, because checkers are partial and get gamed:** rules and tests miss what the designer intended, and agents learn to game them. A check is only as good as its specification, so the question-driven requirements and sourced limits may be the core of the product rather than an add-on.
- **Demand to fix AI-drawn images is unproven:** use is broad but shallow, trust is falling, and I found no professionals relying on AI images. Unchecked answers may be the bigger pain. This is worth testing in user interviews before building on it.
- **Representation:** the research keeps arriving at the same design: a structured intermediate model that gets checked, then written out to DEXPI, Visio or CircuitJSON. That matches the plan of checks on a model plus generated drawings.
- **Competition and pace:** incumbents are adding AI where they already own the checks, and image models improve with each release. Simple devices already work: 60–96% first-try success on 25 common devices in [2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14874), and a working AI-designed board in 2026. Electronics looks crowded; panels, controls compliance and P&IDs look less so.
